# BHUSA26 Conference WLAN Impairment — Single-Sheet Evidence Report

Over two days, the investigation executed more than 250 controlled tests or per-node/per-listener test sessions across multiple conference locations, client cohorts, public transports, packet sizes, rates, ports, and wired/external controls. The distributed phase inventoried 24 probes, established 21 trusted nodes, exercised 21 public listeners across three XMission services, and issued 4,875 synchronized ICMP requests during the 64 KiB, 512 KiB, and optimized bidirectional brackets. The reproducible incident is not general low speed: a client can receive quickly or transmit quickly in isolation, but concurrent upload can starve its download. On the strongest 6 GHz laptop association, HTTP/3 download fell from 679.3 Mbps directional to 41.4 Mbps simultaneous—a loss of 637.9 Mbps of available rate—while HTTP/2 still delivered 333.8 Mbps down and 394.9 Mbps up; generic UDP independently reproduced the same directional signature. The evidence below ranks what the tests establish and what remains unresolved.

| Evidence area | Controlled observation | Finding and evidentiary weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reproduction | Strong 6 GHz/80 MHz radio, approximately -55 to -59 dBm: HTTP/3 delivered 679.3 Mbps down directionally, then 41.4 Mbps with simultaneous upload; later repeats delivered 55.6 and 28.1 Mbps down while the intervening HTTP/2 control delivered 317.0 Mbps. | The complaint was reproduced repeatedly without weak signal or movement. Simultaneous HTTP/3 retained only 6.1% of directional download in the clearest run. **Strong evidence of a real, load-dependent impairment.** |
| Generic UDP direction control | At a 350 Mbps target in each direction, upload delivered 340.11 Mbps with no measured loss while download delivered 273.45 Mbps with 20.771% loss. A recurrence delivered 348.24 Mbps up and 229.90 Mbps down with 33.534% downstream loss. Either direction alone was clean at approximately 350 Mbps. | The trigger requires concurrent bidirectional load and preferentially discards downstream traffic; it is not an HTTP/3-only symptom. **Strong and repeatable.** |
| Rate and packet-size fingerprint | With the opposite direction fixed at 350 Mbps, downstream loss was effectively zero through 250 Mbps, then rose at 300 and 350 Mbps. At 350+350 Mbps, reducing UDP payload from 1,472 to 200 bytes increased downstream loss from 16.303% to 65.119%. | A roughly 250–300 Mbps-per-direction cliff and strong packet-rate sensitivity fit airtime, scheduler, or queue exhaustion better than a simple bandwidth ceiling. **Strong mechanism fingerprint; component not yet identified.** |
| Wired and external controls | On a 1 Gbps conference wired drop, HTTP/3 retained 674.18 of 749.97 Mbps directional download under simultaneous load (89.9%), and six 350+350 Mbps UDP buckets had zero downstream loss. The same laptop on independently operated MGM Wi-Fi did not reproduce the HTTP/3 collapse. | The laptop can operate normally, and the public path is not universally broken. The trigger is absent off the affected conference WLAN. **Strong localization to conference wireless/controller or wireless-VLAN-specific processing.** |
| MSS and path MTU | IPv4 packets at the 1,500-byte path limit succeeded; high-rate TCP transferred without a large-packet black hole. Black Hat MSS values varied by destination rather than showing one blanket clamp. MGM negotiated a smaller MSS yet did not reproduce the collapse. | Missing or incorrect MSS clamping does not explain the directional, concurrency-dependent failure. **MSS/PMTU rejected as the primary cause.** |
| Port, NAT, and dual uplinks | UDP ports 443, 444, and 445 all reproduced the symptom. A live STUN mapping remained stable during failing load; 20 source ports exposed one stable client-visible identity, and fixed-port sweeps did not reveal a healthy/bad hash-bucket population. Aggregate tests were far below the two 10 Gb uplinks. | One blocked QUIC port, mid-session NAT rebinding, raw WAN exhaustion, or one obvious bad client-visible hash bucket is unlikely. Different wired and wireless public identities mean VLAN-specific firewall/NAT/egress or member selection is **not fully excluded** without network telemetry. |
| Distributed fixed-rate cohort | At 100 Mbps offered in each direction, 11 Wi-Fi 5/VHT probes averaged 27.02% downstream loss—about 27.02 Mbps discarded and 72.98 Mbps retained per 100 Mbps offered—while eight Wi-Fi 6/HE probes averaged 0.98% loss, about 0.98 Mbps discarded and 99.02 Mbps retained. Upstream loss averaged 0.095% and 0%, respectively. All associated on 5 GHz/40 MHz. | The asymmetric impairment exists across rooms and VLANs and correlates strongly with the older cohort at a fixed offered rate. **Strong fleet-level correlation, not proof of Wi-Fi 7 backward-compatibility or firmware causation.** |
| Cohort confounders and normalization | The older clients were MediaTek MT7612U/mt76x2u, kernel 5.10, and Wi-Fi 5; newer clients were Intel AX200/AX210/iwlwifi, kernel 6.1, and Wi-Fi 6. No tested probe negotiated Wi-Fi 7/EHT. When scaled to different usable capacities, PC6 at 60+60 Mbps lost 1.670% downstream and PV03 at 125+125 Mbps lost 0.578%. | Generation, adapter, driver, kernel, and PHY capacity move together. Much of the dramatic 100+100 split is airtime/capacity related, although legacy-client downstream efficiency remains materially worse. **Vendor or firmware attribution remains unproven.** |
| TCP control | PC6 delivered 99.9 Mbps down directionally, but only 69.8 and 61.0 Mbps during two 100+100 Mbps TCP runs, with 76 and 56 downstream retransmissions; upload stayed at 100 Mbps. PV03 retained 100–101 Mbps both ways with zero retransmissions. | Congestion control converts the UDP loss into a 30.1–38.9 Mbps download-rate reduction. **Confirms the fault is not limited to UDP, QUIC, or STUN.** |
| Near-side latency | During PC6's 100+100 UDP failure, local-gateway average RTT rose from 1.646 to 7.146 ms and maximum RTT reached 22.738 ms while downstream loss reached 23.550%. PV03 moved only from 2.340 to 2.975 ms with 0.400% downstream loss. Under maximum unbounded bidirectional stress, all six representative clients showed large local-gateway latency increases, up to 184.765 ms, with stable radio signal and zero gateway loss. | Queueing is visible before traffic leaves the client-facing wireless path. Maximum stress can starve downlink on both generations and is a bufferbloat stress result, separate from the controlled fixed-rate cohort defect. **Strong WLAN-side localization; exact drop point unknown.** |
| Public endpoint limits | The synchronized 21-listener fanout completed on the same 12 assignments at both 128 and 64 KiB; pruning the nine repeat timeouts produced 12/12 completion at 512 KiB. Open-port checks had passed before fanout. | The nine failures were public-service admission limits, not zero-throughput client results. Public tests require directional preflight and qualified durations; the repeatable endpoint pattern is documented and excluded from WLAN conclusions. |
| Root-cause assessment | The failure follows concurrent wireless load, is downstream-biased, rate- and packet-pressure-sensitive, crosses UDP and TCP, appears across distributed probes, inflates the local gateway, and disappears on the wired and external controls. Host interface counters did not report errors or drops, but privileged station retry counters were frozen and unusable. | **Most likely fault domain:** client-facing airtime/downlink scheduling, WMM queueing, controller tunnel/datapath, or a legacy-client efficiency interaction. **Not supported as primary causes:** MSS/PMTU, QUIC alone, one UDP port, idle RF loss, roaming, raw 20 Gb WAN capacity, or the public server alone. |

The defensible conclusion is that affected conference WLAN clients encounter a repeatable downstream queueing/starvation condition when upload and download overlap; current evidence places the impairment on or adjacent to the wireless datapath, but it cannot yet distinguish normal legacy-client airtime limits from C-460 scheduling, controller behavior, Wi-Fi 7 backward-compatibility, or wireless-VLAN egress policy. The decisive next run is the same directional and simultaneous PC6/PV03 rate matrix against an authorized internal wired iperf3 endpoint, synchronized with each probe’s AP/radio identity and live C-460 per-client retry, MCS, airtime, WMM queue-drop, controller-tunnel, and AP-uplink counters. Reproduce internally with corresponding AP/controller counter movement and the WAN, NAT, dual uplinks, and public server are excluded; then repeat one matched adapter against C-460 radios in normal Wi-Fi 7 mode and controlled non-BE/earlier-firmware mode before assigning a firmware or backward-compatibility root cause.
